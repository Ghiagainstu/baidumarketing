# 8 Key Differences Between Baidu and Google PPC Advertising

If you're a foreign business looking to advertise in China, you might think Google AdWords experience translates directly to Baidu. It doesn't. The two platforms are built for different markets, different users, and different regulatory environments.

Here are the 8 differences that matter most when running Baidu PPC campaigns.

## 1. Ad Formats and Display Styles

**Baidu's Product Window** vs **Google's Image+Text**

Baidu offers a "product window" style that displays a large image alongside text. The format includes a logo image and multiple site links. Google uses a more conservative image+text style.

The key difference: Baidu can display **2 ads simultaneously** from the same advertiser in one impression. You can activate or deactivate this setting in the Baidu backend. Both the image and site links are charged at CPC, same as keywords. Baidu simply gives you more ad style choices.

## 2. Brand-Exclusive Products: Baidu Brand Zone

Baidu offers a premium "Brand Zone" product that Google doesn't have. This product covers a large portion of the first screen with visual impact. It's expensive, triggered only by brand keywords, and charged by CPT (Cost Per Time).

The price is quoted by Baidu based on search volume. Customized formats cost even more. If you purchase Brand Zone, Baidu typically bundles it with placement in Baidu Knowledge and other Baidu properties.

## 3. Non-Brand Products: Knowledge Marketing & Landmark

**Knowledge Marketing** (aka paid Q&A) appears when users search question-style terms. Ads show on the right side of results pages, charged by CPC. When clicked, users go to a Baidu Q&A page, leveraging the platform's trust factor.

**Landmark** ads look similar to Knowledge Marketing but appear for non-brand terms and are charged by CPT. Landmark offers image and video styles. The format shown in screenshots is typically multi-image.

## 4. Display Products: Baidu In-Feed Ads

Baidu displays native ads in feeds under the search box on mobile devices. These ads mix with organic content across Baidu's ecosystem: Baidu Mobile App, Baidu Mobile Home Page, Baidu Forum, Baidu News, and more.

This is Baidu's answer to social feed advertising, but with search intent behind it.

## 5. Ad Copy Restrictions Are Stricter

Baidu has tighter ad copy rules:
- Limited symbols allowed
- "Official" can't be used unless you prove ownership
- Superlatives like "most" and "best" are prohibited
- Display URLs must match the exact domain you applied for (e.g., google.com, not google.com/cn)

The domain restriction prevents subdomain variations in display URLs.

## 6. Account Opening Is More Complicated

Google account setup is simple. Baidu requires extensive documentation:

1. Business registration (with color stamp of company chop and handwritten statement)
2. Industry license for special industries (finance, food, etc.)
3. Screenshot showing company details from government registry with URL
4. Website homepage screenshot with URL and company chop
5. Authentication proof: Fortune 500 listing, Alexa Top 500, bank account proof, or listed company status

If your documents aren't in Chinese, you need certified translations. Overseas advertisers must remove all Mainland China information from their websites before submission.

## 7. Campaign Settings: PC vs Mobile Preference

Baidu has two campaign types: PC preference and mobile preference.

In PC preference campaigns, you can adjust mobile bidding ratios to minimize mobile traffic. Mobile campaigns work similarly in reverse. This gives you granular control over device targeting.

## 8. Account Access Requires Chinese Phone Number

Baidu only offers admin-level access. For security, they require a Chinese phone number. When login location or device changes, Baidu sends a verification code to that number.

Google offers multiple access levels (admin, standard, read-only). Baidu keeps it simple but restrictive.

## Why These Differences Matter for International Advertisers

Baidu isn't just "Google in Chinese." It's a platform built for China's unique market:

- **Search behavior differs**: Chinese users often search question-style
- **Mobile-first market**: Baidu's mobile features are more advanced
- **Regulatory complexity**: Documentation requirements reflect China's business environment
- **Visual preferences**: Chinese users respond to richer ad formats

The platform differences mean you can't simply translate Google campaigns. You need keyword research specific to Chinese search terms, creative that works within Baidu's restrictions, and understanding of how Chinese users interact with ads.

## Getting Started with Baidu PPC

Foreign businesses typically need:
- A Chinese business license OR work with a registered agent
- Documentation prepared according to Baidu's requirements
- Keyword research in Chinese (not direct translations)
- Creative tailored to Baidu's formats
- Understanding of Baidu's bidding and targeting options

Most international companies work with agencies who handle the compliance layer while focusing on strategy and performance.

## Conclusion

Baidu and Google serve different markets with different user behaviors. Baidu's stricter compliance, richer ad formats, and China-specific features mean you need a China-specific strategy.

Successful Baidu advertising requires understanding these platform differences and adapting your approach accordingly.
