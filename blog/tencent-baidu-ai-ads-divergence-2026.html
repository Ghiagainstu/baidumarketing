Tencent Ads +19%, Baidu Ads −22%: Two Opposite AI Mechanisms

Nov 21, 2026 7 min Market Insights Baidu PPC Pro Team Updated Nov 21, 2026

Two Chinese internet companies published third-quarter results two days apart. Tencent said Marketing Services grew 19% year on year. Baidu said Online Marketing Services fell 22%. Both companies discussed AI in the same set of disclosures, but they did not describe the same mechanism.

For overseas brands, the useful question is not which company has the better AI story. It is where AI sits in the path from user attention to advertising revenue. Tencent described AI close to ad recommendation and insertion. Baidu described AI as a rebuild of the search experience, with monetisation deliberately held back.

The headline numbers start the comparison; they do not prove causation. The 19% and 22% figures are whole-segment year-on-year changes, not AI-attributable gains or losses.

📊 One quarter, two opposite directions

Both reports cover the calendar quarter ended 30 September 2026, and both figures below are in renminbi. That makes the timing and currency comparable. It does not make the business mix identical.

Tencent published its results on 18 November. Alongside the growth figure, it named higher spending from consumer-goods and local-services advertisers. Baidu published on 16 November. Online Marketing Services fell to 48% of General Business revenue, from 62% a year earlier.

These are company-level outcomes, not account-level benchmarks. A foreign advertiser cannot infer its own conversion rate, auction price or return on ad spend from either percentage.

🎯 Tencent puts AI near the point of monetisation

Tencent's release identifies three operational developments around Marketing Services. It improved AI-driven advertising and content recommendation. It introduced an AI-powered tool for content publishers that automates ad insertion and optimises placement timing and duration. It also deepened closed-loop marketing across Weixin, including Mini Dramas and Mini Shops.

The first two items place AI inside the delivery system: choosing content or ads, finding a moment to insert an ad, and deciding how long it appears. The third connects that delivery system to transactions inside Weixin. In analytical terms, this is AI applied close to the point where attention becomes paid inventory.

That distinction matters because the commercial loop is already visible. A publisher has content, an advertiser has demand, and the system adjusts recommendation or insertion between them. AI is presented as an optimisation layer within an existing advertising market.

But Tencent does not disclose a standalone AI contribution to Marketing Services growth. The release lists AI capabilities, closed-loop integration and advertiser demand together. Assigning the full 19% to AI would remove the other disclosed drivers and invent a breakdown Tencent did not publish.

🧭 Baidu puts AI ahead of near-term monetisation

Baidu's release describes a different order of operations. Its AI Search transformation changes how answers are organised and how users move through a query. Management said it would prioritise user experience over near-term monetisation at this stage.

The financial disclosure shows that this is not a painless transition. Online Marketing Services declined 22% to RMB11.9 billion. AI-native Marketing Services, reported in the separate AI-powered business table, declined 15% to RMB2.4 billion. Baidu attributed the AI-native marketing decline to a challenging macroeconomic environment, changing competition and the strategic decision to prioritise user experience.

In analytical terms, Baidu is rebuilding the discovery layer before maximising the advertising yield of that layer. The immediate product question is how a user receives an answer or completes a task, not how to insert more paid messages into that interaction.

This is a management account of cause and intent, not an independently measured decomposition. The release does not show what advertising revenue would have been without the AI Search transition. It also gives no specification for new ad formats, positions or prices inside AI answers. Treating it as an ad-product announcement would go beyond the source.

One model asks how AI can improve the allocation of existing advertising opportunities. The other asks how AI can change the discovery experience on which future advertising may depend. Both can be called AI advertising, but they carry different time horizons and different measurement problems.

The contrast is not proof that one approach must win. Tencent's growth does not establish that every AI insertion tool improves advertiser returns. Baidu's decline does not establish that its search transition will fail. A quarterly revenue line cannot settle either question.

🚫 What the comparison does not establish

Four reporting boundaries prevent a clean comparison from becoming a false causal story.

  • No AI-only growth rate. Tencent's 19% is the growth of the whole Marketing Services segment. Baidu's 22% decline covers its whole Online Marketing Services line. Neither is an AI-attribution metric.
  • No common advertising definition. Tencent's segment and Baidu's reporting lines sit inside different business structures. The figures can be compared as directions, not treated as like-for-like market shares.
  • No arithmetic across Baidu's tables. The RMB13.2 billion AI-powered business, RMB11.9 billion Online Marketing Services and RMB2.4 billion AI-native Marketing Services are different reporting lines. They cannot be added or substituted, and the release does not establish that AI-native Marketing Services is a subset of Online Marketing Services.
  • No advertiser-level verdict. Management explanations describe company outcomes. They do not measure what happened to a particular brand, keyword set, industry or audience.

Baidu's AI-powered business figure also covers cloud and applications, not just marketing. Combining it with either advertising line would produce a number Baidu does not report.

🧰 How overseas brands should use the signal

The right response is a measurement plan, not a budget switch based on two headlines.

Keep platform finance separate from campaign evidence. Company revenue tells you about the platform's direction. Your own search terms, qualified leads, conversion rates and sales cycle tell you whether the platform works for your brand. Review both, but never substitute one for the other.

Match the channel to the user task. Baidu Search can capture explicit Chinese-language demand. Social and content inventory can create or redirect demand in a different context. Treat them as different jobs in a media plan rather than interchangeable containers for AI.

Run controlled tests before drawing conclusions. Hold the offer, landing page, geography and conversion definition constant when comparing campaigns. Without that discipline, a platform narrative becomes a convenient explanation for unrelated changes in the account.

Prepare for answers as well as clicks. A stable Chinese brand name, consistent product facts, dated claims and a locally accessible website make a brand easier to verify across search surfaces. These assets are useful whether the next interaction produces a blue link, an answer or an agent-led task.

Resolve market-entry constraints before the test. Overseas companies still need account access, usable Chinese landing pages and compliant campaign materials. BPP helps international brands set up and operate Baidu advertising without requiring them to build a Chinese legal entity first, so a test measures demand rather than administrative delay.

The practical lesson is narrower and more useful than a winner-and-loser story. Tencent's release describes AI improving an existing monetisation system. Baidu's describes AI reshaping the system that precedes monetisation. For an advertiser, the decision still has to be made at the level of intent, inventory and measured account results.

What to remember

  • The 19% and 22% figures are whole-segment year-on-year changes, not AI-attributable gains or losses.
  • Tencent does not disclose a standalone AI contribution to Marketing Services growth.
  • For an advertiser, the decision still has to be made at the level of intent, inventory and measured account results.
