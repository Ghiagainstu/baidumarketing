AI Apps Hit 546 Million Users in China as Ad Spend Grew 3.1%

Nov 9, 2026 11 min Market Insights Baidu PPC Pro Team Updated Nov 9, 2026

One report, published on 3 November 2026, contains two numbers that point in different directions. AI-native apps in China reached 546 million monthly active users in September, up 82.1 percent year on year. Internet advertising spend in the third quarter reached 193.24 billion yuan, up 3.1 percent.

The first number describes where attention is moving. The second describes where budgets are. For a brand outside China planning a 2027 budget, the distance between them is the actual planning problem.

Most coverage of this report has taken the advertising figure as the headline and treated the AI figures as context. We read it the other way round. The AI figures say something concrete about how buying questions are now being asked in China. The advertising figure says the money has not followed yet. Neither number, on its own, tells a foreign brand what to do.

📊 546 million AI-native app users, with the base stated

As of September 2026, AI-native apps had 546 million monthly active users, up 82.1 percent year on year. The growth rate is applied to a stated base of 546 million, so it can be read as a quantity rather than a headline: the report says the category added 246 million net users in the second quarter alone.

Two other entry layers are larger or faster, and they are not the same thing:

  • Device-maker AI applications: 783 million users, up 16.9 percent.
  • System-level AI applications: 592 million users, up 23.0 percent.
  • PC clients: 26 million users, up 82.3 percent, with usage depth growing 167.7 percent.

Usage depth confirms the shift. AI-native apps reached 88.2 sessions per user per month. Doubao recorded 72.0 sessions and 132.5 minutes per user per month, DeepSeek 65.9 sessions and 136.7 minutes, Yuanbao 38.7 sessions and 78.6 minutes.

The wider market is not growing at that speed. Mobile internet monthly active users stood at 1.281 billion in September, up 0.9 percent. Monthly time per user passed 200 hours for the first time in August, up 7.2 percent, and held at 189.8 hours in September, up 6.5 percent. The report's own framing is that competition has moved from adding users to occupying time and occupying the starting point of a decision. That is the report's characterisation, not our forecast.

💰 Ad spend: 193.24 billion yuan, up 3.1 percent

The third-quarter internet advertising market was 1,932.4 yi yuan, which converts to 193.24 billion yuan. The conversion matters: one yi yuan is 0.1 billion yuan, so the figure in billions is one tenth of the figure in yi. It grew 3.1 percent year on year. This is a single-quarter figure, not an annual total.

We did not use a quarterly series for Q1 and Q2 that circulates in third-party report summaries. Those summaries do not state the methodology or reproduce the report's own quarterly figures, so the series cannot be cross-checked against the report text. The quarterly series was not used, and no full-year figure is inferred from the third quarter here.

Chart figures in press coverage add detail. Hard-advertising advertisers reached 8,356 in the quarter, against 7,594 a year earlier, up 10.0 percent. The top ten media took 86.2 percent of hard-ad spend. Short drama and short video together accounted for 111.5 percent of net time growth, meaning they offset declines elsewhere. Food and beverage hard-ad spend was 9.477 billion yuan, up 11.3 percent quarter on quarter. These are as reported in coverage of the report's charts, not numbers written out in the public report text.

It is worth noticing that the advertiser count grew faster than the market. That suggests more advertisers dividing a slower-growing pool. This is an inference, not a reported conclusion; the report does not publish average spend per advertiser, and we do not assert one. No effect on cost per click, approval rates or campaign performance is quantified, because none has been published.

🔗 Each AI platform now cites sources differently

Section 2.8 of the report is the part most relevant to a brand that wants to appear in AI answers. It describes how the major assistants are changing their citation logic: Doubao and Qwen are cutting back to improve efficiency, Yuanbao and DeepSeek are iterating steadily, Kimi is widening coverage, and Wenxin is expanding in a balanced way. The report calls this a trade-off between source breadth, content precision and answer quality.

Read plainly, this means there is no single set of sources that works across China's AI platforms. A content programme built for one assistant's retrieval behaviour does not automatically serve another's. That conclusion is ours, drawn from the report's description of divergent citation behaviour; it is not a finding the report states.

The report also says the default habit of reaching for an assistant is not yet established, and that phone-maker assistants still depend on being invoked. In planning terms, the entry point is not settled. It is a hypothetical planning scenario, not a reported finding, to assume today's platform mix will still be the mix a brand needs to monitor in twelve months.

One figure that is sometimes read as evidence of a broader trend should stay narrow: 23.591 million university students used AI apps in September, tied to the back-to-school marketing window. It is a seasonal node measurement, not a demographic forecast.

🚫 What the report does not say

The report does not describe any change to ad formats, ad positions or ad pricing. It is a consumer-side and market-level report. AI-native app users, device-maker AI applications and system-level AI applications are user-side categories, not advertising inventory. A reader who concludes that Baidu's ad products changed because AI usage grew would be adding a claim the source does not carry.

A related figure is often quoted in the same coverage: Baidu Group's deduplicated total users, 1.093 billion, down 1.8 percent year on year. That number appears in press reports describing the report's charts, not in the public report text. It is a group-wide deduplicated count across apps and mini-programmes. It is not a count of search users, not a count of ad reach, and not evidence that search usage in China fell.

The report publishes no brand-level, country-level or advertiser-category performance data for international brands. No claim is made here about how any individual brand will perform. Where the report lists countries at all, it does so for Chinese companies expanding outward.

🧱 Where international brands hit the wall

The opportunity is a 546-million-user entry layer where product questions are being asked and answered. The obstacles are the same four that have always governed Baidu advertising for a company without a Chinese entity, plus one that is newer. This is a hypothetical planning scenario, not a reported finding: the report does not evaluate overseas brands.

  • An account requires a Chinese business licence. Most overseas brands do not hold one. Baidu PPC Pro opens and manages the account without it, which decides whether a brand can appear in a Chinese auction at all.
  • Chinese-language facts have to exist before an assistant can cite them. An official Chinese brand name, product categories, specifications, qualifications and a plain statement of scope are the raw material. A translated global homepage usually contains none of it.
  • Sources differ by platform. Doubao, Qwen, Yuanbao, DeepSeek, Kimi and Wenxin do not retrieve from the same place, so a single publishing cadence will not cover them.
  • Landing pages and creative still clear review before they run. Claims are checked against permitted phrasing by industry, and an asset that works in another market can be rejected on submission here.

We make no numerical claim about what closing those gaps delivers. The report publishes no such number.

✅ What to check before Q4 planning

  • Ask each of the six major assistants the twenty to fifty questions your buyers actually ask in Chinese, and record whether your brand appears, how it is described, and which sources are cited. Treat it as a baseline to repeat, not a one-off audit.
  • Check whether your Chinese-language product facts are consistent across your own site, Baidu Baike and any third-party material an assistant might retrieve.
  • Separate your search budget from any work on AI answer visibility. The report's 3.1 percent market growth describes the first; the 82.1 percent user growth describes the second. They are not the same line item.
  • Keep the two evidentiary levels separate in any internal deck. Report-text figures and chart figures in press coverage should not be presented as equally verified.
  • Do not treat the third-quarter advertising figure as a full-year run rate, and do not project the AI user growth rate forward without a stated base.

No claim is made here about how any individual brand will perform, and no applicability to a specific country, language or category is asserted. The report names none for inbound advertisers.

Three figures to carry into planning

  • AI-native apps: 546 million MAU, up 82.1 percent — a stated base, not a headline.
  • Q3 ad spend: 193.24 billion yuan, up 3.1 percent — a single quarter, not a full year.
  • Citation logic differs across Doubao, Qwen, Yuanbao, DeepSeek, Kimi and Wenxin.
