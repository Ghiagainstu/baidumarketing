Baidu's 2026 Upgrades: Why the Opportunity Is Bigger for International Brands — and Harder to Capture

May 2026 5 min read By Baidu PPC Pro Team

The Gap Between Opportunity and Access

There is a pattern we see at BPP. It repeats with nearly every inquiry we receive.

A marketing director at a company based outside China sees Baidu's traffic numbers — 700 million monthly active users on the Baidu App alone, search intent that converts at rates most Western platforms cannot match — and decides to explore. They spend weeks researching, trying to figure out how to get an account running. Somewhere between the Chinese business license requirement, the mainland bank account, the Chinese phone number, and a dashboard that is entirely in Chinese, the project stalls. Six months later, nothing has launched.

Meanwhile, their competitors who found a way in are quietly capturing low-competition keywords, building brand presence in a search engine that 700 million Chinese consumers use every day.

This gap — between the opportunity and the ability to act on it — is the central problem of advertising on Baidu for international brands. And in 2026, it is both wider and narrower than it has ever been.

Baidu's platform has undergone its most significant AI-powered transformation to date. The opportunities are genuinely larger. But the infrastructure needed to access them from overseas has not become simpler. It has, if anything, become more technical.

This article is not a rehash of Baidu's press releases. It is a practical breakdown of what is actually changing, what it means for international brands, and — most importantly — how to get in before the window narrows.

The 2026 Upgrade: What Actually Matters

Baidu invested heavily in AI infrastructure during 2025. The result is a platform-wide shift that touches every product these international brands can use. Three changes in particular are worth your attention.

1. AI-Native Search Advertising

Baidu's search advertising platform has completed a full migration to AI-driven architecture. Campaign setup is now conversational — you describe your business in natural language and the system generates keyword sets, ad copy, and bid strategies. oCPC (optimized cost per click) bidding adjusts in real time based on conversion probability signals. A feature called Smart Refresh automatically diagnoses underperforming campaigns and recommends remediation.

For an international brand, this is deceptively significant. It means much of the technical optimisation work that used to require a Baidu specialist can now be handled by the platform itself. But — and this is the part no one talks about — the platform can only do its job if the account is properly structured from the start, with correct conversion tracking and accurate business data. The AI is powerful, but only with good input.

2. B2B Discovery Platform Expansion

Baidu's AiCaiGou B2B platform — essentially a marketplace and lead generation engine for industrial and enterprise suppliers — has added a serious product layer. An AI-powered Industry Agent now handles technical pre-sales questions automatically. ShangJiTong provides guaranteed minimum lead volumes. A joint membership with Baidu Maps captures buyers who are searching for suppliers geographically before they ever visit your website.

For B2B brands — industrial equipment manufacturers, component suppliers, specialty materials producers — this is a meaningful expansion of addressable demand. But here is the catch: AiCaiGou requires Chinese business credentials. Without a local entity or a trusted account holder, you cannot list.

3. E-Commerce as a Demand Channel

Baidu's e-commerce platform has entered an aggressive growth phase, subsidizing merchant traffic and offering rebate incentives. The HuiBoXing AI digital human tool enables round-the-clock product promotion without live streamers. Early-entering brands are reporting ROI above 1:1.5.

For overseas brands selling products to Chinese consumers — whether directly or through Chinese marketplace partners — this ecosystem is becoming difficult to ignore. But, predictably, it requires a Chinese merchant account.

The Real Problem: It Is Not the Platform. It Is the Credentials.

Every opportunity described above requires one thing: an active Baidu advertising account registered under a Chinese business entity.

Here is what that actually means in practice:

  • Chinese business license (营业执照). Not an overseas subsidiary. A mainland-registered entity.
  • Mainland Chinese bank account. Cross-border payments to Baidu are not straightforward.
  • Chinese mobile phone number. Required for account verification; international numbers are not accepted.
  • A registered local entity or a partner who can hold the account on your behalf.

    • Most international companies have none of these. And even if they do — through a WFOE or joint venture — the operational complexity of managing an account in a fully Chinese-language dashboard, across time zones, with local compliance requirements, is substantial.

    This is the structural barrier that BPP was built to solve. We hold the accounts. We manage the infrastructure. You get the campaigns and the results, without needing to build a China legal entity first.

    Why 2026 Makes This More Urgent

    A poorly-managed Baidu campaign in 2019 could still capture intent-based traffic that converted reasonably well. The platform was simpler. The algorithm was less sophisticated. The gap between a good account and an average one was manageable.

    That gap is now widening fast.

    With AI-driven bidding, smart refresh, and automated campaign generation, the difference between an account that is correctly set up and one that is not is no longer marginal. A campaign running on bad keyword architecture or without proper conversion tracking will bleed budget into irrelevant traffic that the AI system cannot optimise. A campaign that is properly structured, meanwhile, will become progressively more efficient as the AI learns from accumulated conversion data.

    For an international brand, this means two things:

    First, the cost of delay is rising. Every month spent evaluating is a month your competitors are building quality scores, gathering conversion data, and driving their cost per acquisition lower. The gap compounds.

    Second, the cost of getting it wrong is also rising. Launching a Baidu campaign on an improperly structured account today will cost more and perform worse than it would have three years ago. The AI amplifies good decisions — but it also amplifies mistakes.
