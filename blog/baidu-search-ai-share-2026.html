Baidu's AI Search Now Covers 62% of Results — What Fewer Ad Slots Means for Overseas Brands

Jul 22, 2026 6 min Search Ads Baidu PPC Pro Team

If you have been running Baidu search ads for any length of time, you have seen the change. The search results page no longer looks the way it did two years ago. AI-generated answers now dominate the space above the fold, and the traditional five paid ad slots that overseas brands relied on for visibility have been compressed to just two or three.

This is not a gradual shift. Baidu's Q1 2026 marketing data confirms that AI-generated content now covers 62% of search result pages. For international brands investing in Baidu PPC, the implications are immediate and material: fewer positions, higher costs, and a user journey that starts with an AI summary before anyone clicks an ad.

📊 The Hard Numbers: Why This Shift Demands Attention

Three data points from Baidu Marketing Center's Q1 2026 report explain why this structural change matters:

  • 62% AI coverage. AI-generated answers now appear on 62% of Baidu search results pages. Two years ago, this figure was negligible. The AI answer block is now the first thing users see on most commercial queries.
  • CPC up 27.3%, conversion down 18.6%. Average cost-per-click for search ads has risen 27.3% year-over-year, while industry-wide conversion rates have declined 18.6%. The same search budget buys fewer qualified leads than it did twelve months ago.
  • Five slots became three. Paid ad slots on the Baidu search results page have been reduced from a maximum of five to a standard of two or three, all positioned below the AI answer block. If your ad does not land in the top three, it may not appear at all.

The math is straightforward: fewer available slots × higher per-click costs × lower conversion rates = every ad dollar must work significantly harder. Overseas brands without a deliberate adaptation strategy are already watching their return on ad spend compress.

⚠️ What Fewer Ad Slots Actually Means

When five advertisers could appear on a single search results page, a brand with a moderate budget could secure positions four or five and still get meaningful visibility. A manufacturer of industrial equipment, for example, could bid conservatively on "CNC machining supplier" and still appear on page one. That window is closing.

With only two or three slots available, two dynamics have changed fundamentally:

First, the entry barrier is higher. If your quality score and bid combination does not place you in the top three, you may not appear at all — not in position seven or eight, but nowhere on that search query. Second, competition has intensified across the board, pushing CPCs higher as more advertisers chase fewer positions. The brands winning those slots are increasingly those with strong quality scores, not just aggressive bids.

For B2B manufacturers and service providers entering the Chinese market, this creates a genuine dilemma. The Baidu search channel remains the highest-intent audience available — Chinese procurement managers, engineers, and decision-makers use Baidu as their primary research tool. But accessing this audience now requires more sophistication than simply outbidding competitors.

⚠️ Warning: If your quality score and bid do not place you in the top three, you may not appear at all — not in position seven, but nowhere on that query. Outbidding competitors is no longer enough; structural sophistication now decides who gets seen.

🔄 The New User Journey: AI Answers First

The most consequential change is not the slot count — it is how users interact with the results page.

Before AI dominated the Baidu experience, the typical user journey was simple: scan organic results, read several ad headlines, click through to compare. Now, there is an additional, critical step that happens before any ad is seen:

  1. User enters a search query (e.g., "best industrial robot supplier in China").
  2. Baidu's AI generates a structured, multi-paragraph answer at the top of the page, citing specific brands, specifications, and sources.
  3. The user reads this answer and forms an initial judgment — in some cases, making a vendor shortlist decision without ever scrolling further.
  4. Only then does the user scroll past the AI answer to view the two or three paid ads below.
  5. They click an ad primarily to validate or deepen what the AI already told them.

This means the AI answer effectively pre-qualifies your brand. The AI answer has become the first round of a selection process — one that happens without your ad budget, keyword strategy, or landing page being involved at all.

🎯 Adapting Your Baidu Strategy: Three Necessary Shifts

The old playbook — bid aggressively on broad-match head keywords, drive traffic to a conversion-optimized landing page — is not obsolete, but it is no longer sufficient. Three strategic adjustments matter most in the AI-search environment.

To earn AI citations, your brand needs a presence across these Baidu ecosystem surfaces:

🌐 Official Website 📚 Baidu Baike 🏛️ Industry Portals 📰 Press & News

Shift 1: Prioritize AI visibility alongside ad visibility. Baidu's AI model draws from structured, authoritative content to generate its answers. Your brand needs referenceable digital assets that the model can cite: detailed product specifications, technical certifications, case studies with measurable results, industry white papers, and official quality certifications. Content that reads like a marketing brochure does not get cited by AI. Content that reads like a reference document does. The more factual, structured, and citable your brand information is across Baidu's ecosystem — official website, Baidu Baike entries, industry portals, press coverage — the more likely your brand surfaces in AI-generated answers, where the user's attention actually starts.

Shift 2: Rethink keyword allocation. Baidu's Q1 data reveals that most advertisers still allocate 80–90% of their budgets to broad-match head keywords. These positions now carry 60% invalid click rates, and conversion costs are 5–8 times higher than long-tail alternatives. Reallocating 50–70% of budget to long-tail, intent-specific keywords — the actual questions your buyers type into search — typically reduces CPC by two-thirds while improving lead quality. Keywords structured as natural queries (e.g., "ISO-certified CNC machining supplier Guangdong" rather than simply "CNC machining") perform disproportionately well in AI-filtered search environments, because they align with how users formulate questions to AI.

Shift 3: Treat quality score as a gatekeeper, not a tiebreaker. With only two or three ad slots, quality score determines whether you appear at all — not just where you rank. The same keyword pair might cost an advertiser with high quality score 5 RMB for position two, while a lower-quality advertiser pays 10 RMB for position three and receives less visibility despite spending twice as much. Baidu's quality score algorithm increasingly weights domain authority, content freshness, and on-site engagement metrics alongside traditional CTR. A technically maintained website with regularly updated, substantive content is not a branding exercise — it is a direct input to your cost-per-click.

✅ Three Shifts to Make Now

  • Prioritize AI visibility — earn citations in Baidu's AI answers, not just ad positions.
  • Reallocate to long-tail keywords — move 50–70% of budget to intent-specific queries.
  • Treat quality score as a gatekeeper — it decides whether you appear at all.
